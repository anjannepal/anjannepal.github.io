My research focuses on anaerobic soil disinfestation (ASD) in high-tunnel organic vegetable systems, examining how carbon and nitrogen amendments affect weed pressure, yield, and the mineral quality of the subsequent crop.

Research and Extension

Research Projects — Current

Anaerobic Soil Disinfestation for Weed SuppressionCurrent

ASD creates a temporary anaerobic soil environment by incorporating a labile carbon source and sealing the bed under a tarp. It is one of the few non-fumigant options available to organic growers, but its effect on the weed seedbank is far less characterized than its effect on pathogens. I am testing how combinations of carbon and nitrogen amendments impact weed seedbank, weed emergence and composition across a high-tunnel organic vegetable rotation.

Amendment Effects on Yield and Mineral ProfileCurrent

Weed suppression is only useful if the crop that follows is marketable. Alongside the weed work, I track yield and the full mineral profile of organic Tuscan kale grown after ASD, so I can evaluate amendment choices based on both pest pressure and nutritional quality rather than one in isolation.

Research Projects — Past

Heavy Metal Bioaccumulation in Vegetable Crops

Soil amendments that improve fertility can also mobilize trace metals into the crop's edible portion. Across cabbage, mustard, pepper, eggplant, and potato grown under five soil management practices, I quantified Cd, Cu, Zn, and Mo accumulation by ICP-OES and tested hyperaccumulator-based remediation of amended soils. This work produced five peer-reviewed papers.

Extension Training and Activities

BEST Program and FOCCE Scholars

Research on grower practice is only worth doing if it reaches growers. I completed the Broadening Extension through Student Training (BEST) program and the FOCCE Scholars Program, which provided me with the knowledge of extension skills.

Penn State Ag Progress Days, 2025 and 2026

It was a great opportunity to interact with farmers and show them the projects we were conducting in the field. Both years, we showed them the high tunnel Anaerobic Soil Disinfestation (ASD) experiment where we had ASD application, and farmers got a visual idea of how ASD looks in the field. I briefed them about how ASD applications are done, the objectives behind the experiment, and the benefits farmers get from such soil treatment.

ASD Application on a Grower's High Tunnel Infested with Verticillium dahliae

My advisor was advised to meet this farmer who has Verticillium dahliae infestation in his high tunnel. So we visited the high tunnel, which was in Lancaster, PA. We did ASD applications with wheat middlings and molasses in the farmer's high tunnel, took soil samples for initial inspection, and installed ORP sensors to check soil redox potential. We are still awaiting the results from the soil inspection report.

ASD On-Farm Demonstration

We conducted an ASD demonstration on a small farm called Braddock Farms, Pittsburgh, PA. We interacted with the local farmers about the challenges in high tunnels for growing vegetable crops. We first informed them about ASD, which can solve problems of soil-borne pests and pathogens including weeds in high tunnels and provide soil health benefits too. Later, we conducted an ASD application demonstration to show the grower how it is done in the high tunnel.
