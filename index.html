Anjan Nepal

PhD Student, Agriculture and Environmental Plant Science
The Pennsylvania State University

I study how growers can suppress weeds and soil-borne pests without synthetic fumigants. My dissertation work centers on anaerobic soil disinfestation (ASD) in high tunnel organic vegetable systems, asking how carbon and nitrogen amendments affect weed pressure, yield, and the mineral quality of the crop that follows.

I work with Dr. Francesco Di Gioia and Dr. Caio Brunharo at Penn State. Before this I spent four years at Kentucky State University studying soil amendments and heavy metal bioaccumulation in vegetable crops, where I ran ICP-OES elemental analysis on a USDA-funded project.

Anaerobic Soil Disinfestation for Weed SuppressionCurrent
PhD dissertation · Penn State · 2025–present

ASD creates a temporary anaerobic soil environment by incorporating a labile carbon source and sealing the bed under tarp. It is one of the few non-fumigant options available to organic growers, but its effect on the weed seedbank is far less characterized than its effect on pathogens. I am testing how carbon and nitrogen amendment combinations change weed emergence and composition across a high tunnel organic vegetable rotation.

Amendment Effects on Yield and Mineral ProfileCurrent
Penn State · presented at ASHS 2026

Weed suppression is only useful if the crop that follows is marketable. Alongside the weed work I track yield and the full mineral profile of organic Tuscan kale grown after ASD, so amendment choices can be evaluated on both pest pressure and nutritional quality rather than one in isolation.

Heavy Metal Bioaccumulation in Vegetable Crops
Kentucky State University · 2021–2025

Soil amendments that improve fertility can also mobilize trace metals into the edible portion of the crop. Across cabbage, mustard, pepper, eggplant, and potato grown under five soil management practices, I quantified Cd, Cu, Zn, and Mo accumulation by ICP-OES, and tested hyperaccumulator-based remediation of amended soils. This work produced five peer-reviewed papers.
